READING 1 | READING 1 — AN ART PROJECT THAT MADE A CITY FEEL LIKE HOME
CHỦ ĐỀ: UNIT 5 ART + UNIT 6 WHERE I’M FROM
SỐ CÂU: 5 | DẠNG LỰA CHỌN: A–C
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BẮT ĐẦU ĐOẠN VĂN (PASSAGE):
  [P1] When Anya moved from a small seaside town to a large metropolis, she missed the quiet streets
  and her old friends. She had been living in her new neighbourhood for four months, but she still did
  not feel at home. One morning, she noticed a poster asking people to help turn a bare wall beside
  the local library into a colourful mural.

  [P2] Anya joined the project because she enjoyed drawing comics. The group included students,
  shopkeepers and a retired art teacher. They first talked to local residents about their memories.
  One woman remembered how the old harbour had looked thirty years earlier. A teenager suggested
  adding pictures of bicycles because many children now travelled to school that way.

  [P3] The volunteers met every Saturday for five weeks. The retired teacher helped them plan the
  design, but everyone was free to contribute ideas. A nearby café donated paint and brushes. At
  first, Anya was worried that her drawings would not be good enough. However, her teammates
  encouraged her, and she created a small cartoon showing different generations sharing the same
  street.

  [P4] When the mural was finished, the library held a free celebration with music from a local choir.
  Neighbours stopped to admire the painting and talked to people they had never met before. The wall
  did not solve the city's problems, but it made the area feel friendlier. Anya says the experience
  helped her make a connection with her new community, and she has been making new friends ever since.

READING 2 | READING 2 — A BETTER BALANCE, ONE SMALL CHANGE AT A TIME
CHỦ ĐỀ: UNIT 7 BALANCE + UNIT 8 ESSENTIALS
SỐ CÂU: 5 | DẠNG LỰA CHỌN: A–C
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BẮT ĐẦU ĐOẠN VĂN (PASSAGE):
  [P1] During her first year at university, Maya thought a productive day had to be a busy day. She
  attended classes, worked in a café, and answered messages late at night. She often forgot to eat
  properly and spent money on fizzy drinks and snacks. Although she was busy, she found it difficult
  to concentrate when she needed to study.

  [P2] Maya decided to make three changes. First, she wrote a short to-do list every morning with only
  three important tasks. Second, she started taking five-minute microbreaks after long periods of
  study. During these breaks, she walked outside instead of looking at her phone. Third, she made a
  weekly budget and prepared simple meals at home rather than buying expensive food every day.

  [P3] Her first week was not easy. She sometimes wanted to check social media during her breaks, and
  making lunch took more time than buying it. However, she noticed that she could finish her homework
  earlier because she was not switching between tasks so often. She also began buying some second-hand
  clothes because they cost less and could reduce waste.

  [P4] Three months later, Maya still works hard, but she no longer believes that working without rest
  is the best way to succeed. She has saved enough money for a short trip and spends more time with
  friends. Her advice is simple: choose a few habits that fit your life and keep practising them. You
  do not need to change everything in one day.

READING 3 | READING 3 — FIVE UNUSUAL WAYS TO TAKE A BREAK
CHỦ ĐỀ: UNIT 9 TAKING A BREAK + UNIT 10 THE SENSES
SỐ CÂU: 5 | DẠNG LỰA CHỌN: A–E (MATCHING INFORMATION)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BẮT ĐẦU ĐOẠN VĂN (PASSAGE):
  A. THE SOUND GARDEN. Visitors follow a short path through trees while listening to water, birds and
  the wind. There are no loud speakers or recorded songs. A guide asks everyone to close their eyes
  for one minute and identify as many natural sounds as possible. The activity is popular with people
  who spend most of their days in noisy offices.

  B. THE COLOUR WALK. In a small historic town, an artist leads visitors through streets filled with
  colourful houses. Participants take photographs and learn how colours can change the atmosphere of a
  place. At the end, they create a simple picture inspired by the buildings they have seen. No
  painting experience is required.

  C. THE DARK DINNER. Guests eat a three-course meal in a room with almost no light. The aim is to
  notice flavour, texture and scent more carefully when they cannot see the food. Before the meal,
  staff explain the safety rules and ask about food allergies. Some visitors find the experience
  surprising, while others feel slightly nervous.

  D. THE CITY SOUND MUSEUM. Instead of showing paintings, this museum collects recordings of ordinary
  life: a market opening, a train arriving and children playing in a square. Visitors listen to short
  clips and guess what they are hearing. They can even record a familiar sound from their own
  neighbourhood for a future exhibition.

  E. THE OFFLINE FOREST CABIN. This quiet cabin has no Wi-Fi or television. Visitors leave their
  phones in a safe box, prepare simple meals and go walking on nearby trails. The owner recommends
  staying for at least two nights because the first day can feel strange without messages and social
  media. After that, many guests say they sleep better and feel more relaxed.
